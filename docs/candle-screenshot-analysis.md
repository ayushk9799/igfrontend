# Candle App Store Screenshot Analysis

Reviewed: 28 July 2026  
Listing: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/candle-couples-relationship/id6743355635

## Executive summary

Candle's screenshots behave like a short advertising campaign rather than a complete feature tour. The sequence moves through four ideas:

1. emotional proof that real couples use and keep the app;
2. a simple daily habit;
3. a collection of activities for two;
4. a concrete home-screen benefit.

The strongest lesson for Penguin Couple is to sell the relationship outcome before presenting the feature inventory. The weakest aspect is Candle's heavy use of ranking, rating, and user-count claims. Penguin should not imitate those unless every claim is current, verifiable, and permitted on the target store.

## Visible screenshot sequence

### 1. “Designed to be kept.”

**Composition**

- Full black background.
- Large white two-line headline at the top.
- A nine-photo collage of real couples occupies most of the frame.
- White laurels and a “#1 couples app globally” claim sit at the bottom.
- No actual app interface is shown.

**What it communicates**

- This is an emotionally meaningful product, not a disposable utility.
- Other real couples already use it.
- The product belongs in the user's relationship and camera roll.

**Why it works**

- Real faces create immediate warmth and identification.
- The headline is unusually concise and memorable.
- Black creates strong contrast against colourful relationship photos.
- It leads with a result—retention and shared memories—not mechanics.

**Weaknesses and risks**

- It does not explain what the app does.
- “#1” is a substantiation and metadata-policy risk, particularly on Google Play.
- The claim may age badly as rankings change.
- A dense collage can feel generic if the photography is not distinctive.

**Penguin lesson**

Use an emotional opening, but keep enough of the home experience visible to make the product understandable. A Penguin mascot/mood hero plus a small real or synthetic couple-photo element would preserve both emotion and product clarity.

### 2. “Play daily challenges”

**Composition**

- Black background with white headline.
- The word “challenges” uses an italic serif typeface, creating editorial contrast.
- A large phone-like panel shows a couple photo.
- A purple “Daily Photo Challenge” notification overlaps the top of the photo.
- Rating, stars, user count, and laurels occupy the bottom.

**What it communicates**

- The app creates a tiny repeatable daily action.
- The action produces personal content rather than abstract progress.
- Participation feels lightweight and social.

**Why it works**

- “Daily” sets a clear usage frequency.
- The notification makes the interaction feel timely and alive.
- The real photo demonstrates the output of the challenge instantly.
- The composition contains only one feature and one action.

**Weaknesses and risks**

- The screenshot uses more social-proof space than product-interface space.
- The visible 4.9 rating does not match the 4.8 rating currently shown on the listing.
- “1M+ users” needs a reliable, continuously maintained source.
- The UI mock is heavily stylized, so the submitted app must still deliver an equivalent experience.

**Penguin lesson**

The Daily Ritual should be Penguin's second screenshot. Show one excellent question card and both partners' avatars. Avoid ratings and user-count claims; use that space for the emotional payoff:

> One question. Better conversations.

### 3. “Fun activities for the two of you”

**Composition**

- Black background and large white headline.
- “the two of you” is italic serif, reinforcing the couples positioning.
- A large cropped app screen fills the lower two-thirds.
- The screen includes a streak, Canvas art, a travel countdown, arcade tiles, and Thumb Kiss.

**What it communicates**

- Candle is not a single-purpose question app.
- There is variety for repeated use.
- Features are organized into playful, visually distinct cards.

**Why it works**

- It proves product depth after the first two emotional frames.
- Multiple tiles create discovery without requiring explanatory labels outside the app UI.
- The interface is shown at a readable scale.

**Weaknesses**

- The screen is visually busy.
- Small activity text may be unreadable on search and recommendation surfaces.
- Several features compete for attention, so no single one becomes memorable.

**Penguin lesson**

Penguin's Games hub can use this structure, but the screenshot headline should name the value clearly:

> Playful challenges for two

Show Jigsaw, Tic-Tac-Toe, and Wordle, with one deliberate “Your turn” state. Do not add extra marketing badges over the cards.

### 4. “Send pics to their homescreen”

**Composition**

- Black background with a direct benefit headline.
- “homescreen” is italic serif.
- A large iOS home-screen crop fills most of the frame.
- A colourful drawing/widget dominates the top.
- A couple photo appears in another widget below.

**What it communicates**

- The relationship becomes visible outside the app.
- A partner can send content directly into the other person's daily phone experience.
- The feature is concrete enough to understand without explanation.

**Why it works**

- This is the clearest benefit-led frame in the set.
- It demonstrates the destination, not just the in-app setup screen.
- Bright widget art stands out strongly against the black frame.

**Weaknesses**

- It is platform-specific and cannot be reused unchanged for Android.
- The headline says “pics,” while the dominant widget looks more like a drawing.
- The heavily blurred home screen slightly reduces product credibility.

**Penguin lesson**

Do not limit Penguin's widget screenshot to the in-app widget library. A stronger frame should show the actual lock/home-screen result:

> Love on your home screen

Use a Penguin Scribble widget, Partner Photo, and either Days Together or Our Distance. Produce separate iOS and Android artwork.

## Candle’s visual system

| Element | Candle approach | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Solid black | Premium, high contrast, cinematic |
| Headline | Large white sans serif | Fast comprehension |
| Emphasis | Italic serif word or phrase | Editorial, romantic personality |
| UI placement | Oversized and cropped from the lower half | Keeps screens readable |
| Photography | Real couples and candid moments | Emotional trust and relatability |
| Colour | Mostly contained inside photos and app UI | Makes product content pop |
| Copy length | Usually 3–7 words | Good for small store surfaces |
| Social proof | Ratings, laurels, ranking, user count | Strong persuasion but high compliance/maintenance risk |
| Narrative | Emotion → daily habit → feature depth → widget utility | Clear progression |

## What Penguin should borrow

- Lead with an emotional benefit instead of “all features in one app.”
- Keep headlines under seven words.
- Make one important word visually distinctive.
- Enlarge the actual app UI and allow intentional cropping.
- Use authentic-looking couple content, but only synthetic, licensed, or consented imagery.
- Demonstrate the output of a feature, not its setup flow.
- Put a highly differentiated feature in the first three frames.
- Create separate widget frames for iOS and Android.

## What Penguin should not copy

- Do not reuse Candle's black-and-white visual identity; Penguin's warm pink, lilac, and blue gradients are more ownable for the existing product.
- Do not copy the serif/sans typography treatment exactly.
- Do not use “#1,” “best,” rating, download, or user-count claims without current proof and a policy review.
- Do not lead with a photo collage that hides Penguin's actual interface.
- Do not crowd one screenshot with every feature.
- Do not show adult question categories in public store artwork.

## Recommended adjustment to Penguin's sequence

The earlier Penguin plan remains valid, but Candle suggests two improvements:

1. Make screenshot one more emotional.
2. Show the real home/lock-screen widget result instead of only the Widget Library.

Updated sequence:

| Order | Headline | Visual |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Your little world for two** | Home mood hero with both penguins and a small couple-photo element |
| 2 | **2,000+ questions for two** | Question-category or question-set library |
| 3 | **Answer. Compare. Connect.** | Question card or completed answer comparison with both avatars |
| 4 | **Draw together, live** | Two-colour shared canvas with both partners present |
| 5 | **Playful challenges for two** | Games hub with Jigsaw, Tic-Tac-Toe, and Wordle |
| 6 | **Keep your story together** | Populated Timeline with photos and special dates |
| 7 | **Love on your home screen** | Actual platform home/lock screen with Scribble, Partner Photo, and relationship widget |
| 8 | **Video chat, just for two** | Connected Video Chat with both partners visible and controls unobstructed |

## Competitive opportunity for Penguin

Candle's screenshots emphasize photos, daily activities, and widgets, but they do not visibly communicate several strengths that Penguin can own:

- combined two-partner mood mascots;
- live shared drawing;
- a fuller games line-up with Jigsaw, Tic-Tac-Toe, and partner Wordle;
- a chronological relationship Timeline;
- live video calling while remaining inside the couple experience;
- broader question categories and formats;
- seven supported app languages.

Penguin should avoid trying to look more prestigious than Candle. It should aim to look warmer, more playful, more obviously interactive, and more feature-rich while keeping every frame focused on one promise.
